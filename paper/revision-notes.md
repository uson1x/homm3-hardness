# Notes on the November 2026 revision

The four-page paper gives the result and its proof ideas. The full paper supplies the constructions, proofs and verification scope. The changes below address a review of the September version.

- **Which game is covered.** The short edition opens with the native seven-slot limit, the prescribed deployment hexes, the one-round deadline and the scripted garrison. The theorem describes worst-case instances of this generalized model. The difficulty of native formations and typical player positions remains open here.
- **Fixed deployments.** Section 2.3 now defines fixed and freely chosen deployments separately. With two 2-HP enemies on separate lanes and two one-damage creatures, a fixed 1+1 split scores zero. A 2+0 split kills one enemy. Corollaries 3.1 and 4.1 establish hardness directly for their prescribed deployments.
- **Certificate checking.** The NP-membership proof uses a board search with a visited set. A reachable hex is visited at most once per search even when speed is written as a large binary integer. The Lean reference checker uses one expansion per numerical unit of speed. Its certificate equivalence establishes correctness. The efficient verifier and its runtime argument are given separately in Section 2.3.
- **Total reductions.** Theorems 2 and 4 now handle malformed number lists by building their respective boards from six damages 4, 4, 4, 6, 6, 6 and a target of 15. All 30 nominal damage points would be needed, and even values cannot sum to 15. This gives a negative instance inside each stated board family. The same normalization covers the prescribed army and hit-point variants in Lean.
- **What Lean checks.** For the single-type construction, the formal proof starts from a valid orthogonal drawing. Appendix D.4 explains how to obtain one from the planar input, citing the drawing theorem and applying it to each connected component. The released checks exercise that interface. Agreement with the engine is supported by selected comparisons. Neither of these is a claim of independent human peer review.
- **What is new.** Earlier work treats attack partitioning in attrition games and exact-sum targeting in Hearthstone. The contribution here is the separation between deployment and targeting within the same specified combat rules, including the way death and melee occupancy implement exact cover. The separate single-run language-model experiment supplies descriptive data. It is outside the paper's complexity claims.
